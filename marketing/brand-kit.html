The problem

Your asset register is a spreadsheet nobody trusts

Most offices, schools, clinics and workshops track equipment in an Excel file one person maintains. It goes stale the week it is made — and the cost shows up later.

Laptops and tools walk out and nobody can say when, or who signed for them.
Auditors ask for proof of purchase and value; the file has neither a photo nor a receipt.
Two branches keep two versions, both wrong, and insurance claims get argued down.
The answer

Label it once. Scan it forever.

1

Print the labels

AssetSnap generates QR label sheets with running asset codes. Stick one on each item — desk, laptop, drill, projector.

2

Scan to capture

Open the app, point the camera, add a photo, location and holder. The record is created in seconds, even with no signal.

3

Know at a glance

Search, filter and export the whole register. Movement history, warranty dates and values, ready for audit or insurance.

Built for how work actually happens

Installs as an app on any phone — no app store, no IT project. Locations nest Floor → Room, so "where is it" has a real answer. Staff get their own logins with roles, so a storekeeper cannot rewrite prices.

Your data stays yours

Each organisation is isolated at the database level. Every edit is written to an append-only audit trail — no route deletes it, at any role. Assets are never hard-deleted. Export to CSV whenever you want.

Brand basics

How AssetSnap should look and sound

The mark

A rounded square (the asset label) with a scan line through it. Keep clear space of at least the mark's own corner radius on every side; never stretch it, never place it on a busy photo. Minimum size 16 px on screen, 6 mm in print.

The wordmark

"AssetSnap" set as one word, heavy weight, tight tracking. "Asset" in ink, "Snap" in brand blue on light backgrounds; "Snap" in teal on dark. Never hyphenate, never all-caps it in body text.

Blue carries the brand and every primary button. Teal is for confirmation and the scan motif only. Amber marks the free trial and anything needing attention — never as a background for body text.

Typography

One sans-serif family throughout — Inter, or Helvetica/Arial as the safe fallback. Headings: extra-bold, tight tracking. Body: regular, 1.5 line height. Numbers in tables right-aligned. No script fonts, no outlines, no drop shadows.

Say it like this
  • Plain, short sentences. Rupees, not "pricing tiers".
  • Concrete nouns: laptop, drill, projector, store room.
  • Claim only what the product does today.
  • Lead with the one tap and the free week.
Not like this
  • "Revolutionary AI-powered asset intelligence platform."
  • Fear-selling or invented statistics.
  • Jargon: synergy, holistic, end-to-end ecosystem.
  • ALL CAPS shouting or stacked exclamation marks.

One line to remember

Anything, anywhere — secured in one tap.

Short forms: "Scan it. Know it." for social; "Your asset register, in your pocket." for print.
